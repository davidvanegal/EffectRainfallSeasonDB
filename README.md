Abstract
Biological diversity can vary in time and space. However, our understanding of how it changes over time remains limited. Seasonality and habitat fragmentation have negative effects on the structure of dung beetle assemblages in the tropics. Nevertheless, it is still unclear how rainfall seasonality influences assemblages in regions such as the Middle Magdalena of Santander. Dung beetles are an ecologically important group of insects that can help us understand the effects of temporality on this type of ecosystem. In this context, the aim of this study was to analyze how rainfall seasonality influences the structure, composition, and taxonomic and functional diversity of dung beetle assemblages. Dung beetle assemblages were analyzed during the rainy and dry seasons in a humid tropical landscape in Puerto Wilches, Santander, Colombia. The results showed that abundance decreased during the rainy season, although species richness remained stable. The functional groups of tunnelers and dwellers, as well as small and large beetles, responded negatively to rainfall. These changes have ecological implications, since shifts in dung beetle assemblages can directly affect the ecosystem services these insects provide. Designing conservation strategies for both these insects and the mammals that supply their food resource is necessary to counteract the negative impacts of anthropogenic activities in humid tropical landscapes such as Middle Magdalena.
Keywords: Scarabaeinae, beta diversity, functional diversity, humid tropical forest, seasonality.
